About

Computer science, graph learning, agent memory, and what drives me.

I did my undergraduate degree in Computer Science at Fudan University, in Shanghai. During my time there, I became deeply interested in how machines represent and reason about information—first through graph structures, and eventually through the lens of memory and agency. Along the way, I published a paper as second author, contributed to open-source infrastructure, and spent time thinking about what it really means for an AI system to remember.

These days, I work as an Agentic AI Engineer at a startup building agent memory systems. I have been designing and building production agent memory architectures from the ground up—focusing on retrieval as a first-class problem and developing approaches for causal reasoning and verification that make memory an active component of agent reasoning.

I’m an incoming MSECS student at Johns Hopkins University, and I plan to keep pushing on the intersection of memory and world models—building agents that don’t just act, but understand.
