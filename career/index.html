What I Harvested

I gained a comprehensive understanding of existing agent memory systems — especially the current bottleneck in the field. Most existing systems are explicit: storing and retrieving structured information through layered, classified retrieval channels to cover different patterns. This approach is practical in engineering, but in my view, it is not the ultimate form of memory.

Explicit memory is not coupled with reasoning. It cannot evolve or continuously learn like human memory. Meanwhile, latent memory faces its own challenges: tight coupling with the inference side makes transfer between models difficult. I attempted to replicate a well-known open-source latent memory system and found methodological issues — it relies on attention computation in context rather than actual latent space compression.

However, in world models — especially embodied scenes and long video generation — memory is native. These scenarios are inherently time-sensitive and non-Markovian. Effective memory can prevent long-horizon drift in video generation, assist embodied AI in completing temporal-dependent tasks, and enhance controllability.
